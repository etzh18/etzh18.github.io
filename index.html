Research

Ongoing · Cornell University & EXEA Labs · Sole author

“Messi” Business: The Effect of Clean Zones, Crowds and Closures at the 2026 World Cup

FIFA asked every 2026 World Cup host city to set up “Clean Zones” that limit signs and street vendors near stadiums and fan festivals. Business owners blamed the zones and the road closures for lost sales. I'm testing which one, if either, actually hurt them, across all 16 host cities.

Early results: the zone rules did not measurably cut sales. Crowds and spending went up. Where owners did report losses, news coverage tied them to closures and parking bans, not the zone rules.

16 host cities 1,310 Texas bars geocoded Boston Fan Fest bike trips +19% (p = .004) Host-city card spending ~+5%

Methods: synthetic difference-in-differences, a border comparison of bars just inside and outside each zone, and placebo tests. Every analysis plan was locked in before I looked at the data. Built in Python with sales, bike share, subway, card spending and ICE arrest data.

Inclusive or Selective? Evaluating Fintech Credit Allocation under Quantitative Tightening in Urban Low-Income and SME Communities in the U.S.: A Case Study of Upstart

When the Fed started shrinking its balance sheet in 2022, borrowing got more expensive and lenders got pickier. We looked at whether Upstart, an AI lending platform, kept lending to small businesses and low-income borrowers anyway. It did: by selling about 65% of its loans to investors through securitization, it moved default risk off its books and kept credit flowing.

Advances in Economics, Management and Political Sciences 236(1): 251–259
